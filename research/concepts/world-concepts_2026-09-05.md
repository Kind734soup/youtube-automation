Read this carefully: **cozy shelter is the most crowded category (n=19) and has the lowest
median views (57.8k).** High engagement there reflects a small, loyal audience, not reach.
Transit tops views/day on **n=2** — a suggestive but thin sample.

## Two hypotheses the data did NOT support

Checked before building concepts, and both came back negative — recorded so they are not
re-proposed later:

1. **Seasonal titling as a growth lever.** On Calmed By Nature, seasonal-titled videos
   median **260,079** views vs non-seasonal **457,925** (n=22 vs n=3). Seasonal framing did
   not outperform. A "winter world" is not supported as a reach play.
2. **Problem-framing ("Stop Overthinking", "If anxiety keeps you awake") as a big lever.**
   On Get Sleepy: problem-framed median 29,469 views / 2.84% ER vs plain 24,389 / 2.79%.
   A real but modest lift — worth using in the title, not worth building a world around.

## Rank 1 — The Night Train Through the Ages  ← RECOMMENDED

**Premise.** A sleeper train that never quite arrives. The listener has a private compartment;
each time they look out of the window, the train is passing through a different age — a
Roman aqueduct at midnight, a lamplit Victorian platform, a steppe under Bronze Age stars.
Nothing is explained and nothing is at stake. The corridor, the compartment, and the sound
of the rails stay constant while the world outside changes.

**Scene spine (8 scenes, matching the Egypt build):**
1. Boarding — an empty platform, warm lamplight, luggage settled, the train exhaling
2. The compartment — blanket, small lamp, condensation on cold glass, rails finding rhythm
3. First window — moonlit aqueduct and cypress, an old road running beside the track
4. The dining car — empty, laid for no one, brass and green glass, tea going cold
5. Second window — a Victorian station drifting past without stopping, gaslight and steam
6. The corridor at the deepest hour — every other door dark, floor swaying underfoot
7. Third window — open steppe, no lights anywhere, stars all the way to the ground
8. Not arriving — the rails slow but never stop; the lamp dims; sleep before the station

**Why this ranks first.**
- Fuses the two strongest signals: transit spine (highest median views/day, 1,731) with a
  history payload (highest engagement, 28.8/1k; proven in-house with the Egypt world).
- The two night-train datapoints are both strong performers, not one fluke:
  *Rainy Night Train of Ages* (1,956/day) and *Night Train to Scotland* (193,023 views,
  1,506/day) — the latter is the 2nd-highest view total in the whole idea set.
- **Solves a real production problem.** `style_guide.md` demands gentle transitions and a
  "continuous, unbroken journey." The Egypt world had to hard-cut between the Nile, Giza,
  the Duat and a temple hall. A train makes every transition diegetic — the window changes
  by itself. The style guide is satisfied by the premise rather than by careful writing.
- Reusable format. "Night Train" becomes a series shell; each future world is a new route,
  reusing compartment/corridor/dining-car art across uploads.

**Risks.** Transit evidence is n=2. Not a keyword-search category the way "Ancient Egypt" is,
so it leans on browse/suggested rather than search. Mitigate with a search-legible title,
e.g. *Fall Asleep on a Night Train Through History — Rome, Victorian London and the Silk Road*.

## Rank 2 — Fall Asleep to the ENTIRE Story of Ancient Rome

**Premise.** A straight replication of the Egypt format on the next-most-searched ancient
civilisation. Dusk over the Forum, a lamplit insula stairwell, the baths after closing,
a merchant galley at anchor, a legionary road at night, a villa garden before dawn.

**Why it ranks here.** The safest option on the board. "ENTIRE story" long-form has the
highest median view count in the set (164,523), our pipeline has already executed exactly
this shape end-to-end, and "Ancient Rome" carries real search volume. Every unknown is a
known.

**Why not first.** It is a repeat, and it competes head-on with Bedtime & Historian, whose
Egypt video (164,523 views, 1,565/day) is the direct model for our own. Being the second
channel to the same shelf is a weak position, and it teaches us nothing new about the audience.

## Rank 3 — The Tea Shop at the End of the Lane (Victorian cozy-institution)

**Premise.** A shop that is somehow always open and always expecting you. Rain on the glass,
the copper urn, a cat on a chair by the fire, a back room of shelves that go further than the
building should allow.

**Why it ranks here.** Best pure community signal in the whole dataset: *The Tea Shop That
Knew What People Needed* draws **71.1 comments per 10k views**, roughly 2.5× the set median.
Comments feed the algorithm and build a returning audience.

**Why not higher.** Lowest reach ceiling. This sits in the cozy-shelter category — 19 of 25
videos, the most saturated on the board, and the lowest median views (57.8k). Good for
retention and loyalty; poor for growth, which is what a *new world* is for.

---

## Recommendation

**Build the Night Train.** Rome is the safer number, but it buys a known outcome at the cost
of ever differentiating; the train takes the format that already works for us and adds the
one structural element the style guide keeps asking for and the Egypt build had to fake.

If the appetite is for certainty over upside, Rome is the correct fallback and needs no
further research — the pipeline can execute it as-is.
